Shotcut: the happy pathField Guide #2 · turn your picked beats into a reel, start to finish

LosslessCut is the paper cutter — fast, rough, one job. Shotcut is the workbench — where beats become a reel. This is the whole path: set the canvas, lay down your clips, keep the good parts, export. Nothing else.

1. Set the canvas first

Before you touch a single clip: Settings → Video Mode → choose the vertical 9:16 HD preset (1080×1920, 30fps). This is the size of your finished reel.

Do this first, not later. Changing the video mode after clips are on the timeline scrambles your layout. Canvas first, clips second — always.

2. Get clips on the timeline

Open your source video (Open File, or just drag it in from Double Commander). Drag it down onto the timeline. One long clip is fine — you're about to carve it up.

Press Space to play, ← → to nudge. Find your first beat.

3. Keep the good, cut the rest

Park the playhead where a beat starts and press S — that splits the clip at the playhead. Move to where the beat ends, press S again. Now the beat is its own piece.

Click the junk pieces and press Delete. Drag the keepers together so there are no gaps. Repeat for beat two, beat three.

Zoom the timeline with the slider at the bottom if your beats are short — trying to split a 3-second moment on a zoomed-out timeline is an exercise in frustration.

Bonus: framing with Size & Zoom, then Crop

Need to tighten the framing — cut a distracting edge out, or zero in on the subject? Two filters, in this order:

First — fill the canvas. If your video is smaller than the black frame around it, click the clip on the timeline, open Filters, hit +, go to Video, and pick Size, Position & Rotate. Raise the Zoom slider until the video fills the canvas edge to edge. No black bars left.

Then — crop. With the clip still selected, hit + again and pick Crop: Rectangle. A rectangle appears over your video in the preview — drag its corners to frame what you want to keep. Everything outside it gets cut away. The Left / Top / Right / Bottom numbers underneath do the same thing precisely, in pixels.

Why this order: cropping a video that's floating small inside a big black canvas just gives you a smaller floating video. Zoom fills the frame first, then the crop decides which slice of the scene survives.
Reel framing trick: drag the crop rectangle onto the subject, not the middle of the frame — the bird decides the framing, not the geometry.

4. Save the project

Ctrl+S, give it a name. Shotcut has autosave, but the two seconds this takes have saved many afternoons. Save before you export, every time.

5. Export the reel

Click Export in the top toolbar. In the export panel:

→ Make sure the resolution reads 1080×1920 (it follows your video mode).
→ Set the quality to 55%.
→ Click Export File, name it, and watch the Jobs panel on the right.

Learned the hard way: 70% quality on a 50-second clip produced a 168 MB monster. For Instagram reels, 55–60% is the sweet spot — looks identical on a phone, fraction of the size. Hardware encoding (QuickSync on your machine) is fine for drafts.
Putting the watermark on? That's an ffmpeg job after export, not a Shotcut job — the watermark gets stamped after the final resize, at 80px wide, top-left for reels. One command, done in seconds.
