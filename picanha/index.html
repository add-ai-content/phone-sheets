🥩 Picanha, Three Ways

One picanha (about 3 lb), three finishes: a smoked roast, a chimichurri steak, and a churrasco skewer. First cooked Nov 27, 2026 — this page is the playbook so the next one goes the same way, only smoother.

The cut read the SIDE view, not the top

Lay the picanha lean-side up, fat cap underneath. Judge the thickness from the side: top-down, the fat cap bulges toward you and lies about which end is thick.

Tip endthinnest third
→ Steak 2, the skewer
Centermiddle third
→ Steak 1, chimichurri
Thick endabout 40%
→ the Roast
  • The pale natural seam sits just tip-side of the roast cut — leave it with the roast.
  • The thin tip folds easiest, which is why it gets the skewer.
  • Cut the pieces with the grain. Every final serving slice goes against the grain.
  • Never trim the fat cap. It is the whole point.

Chimichurri cilantro + parsley — verdict: brilliant

  • ½ cup flat-leaf parsley + ½ cup cilantro, hand-chopped (never puréed)
  • 3–4 garlic cloves, minced
  • 1 tsp dried oregano · ½ tsp red chili flakes
  • ⅓ cup olive oil · 2 Tbsp red wine vinegar
  • Salt and black pepper; optional small shallot and a squeeze of lime

Rest at least 30 minutes before serving — an hour is better. The 50/50 parsley-cilantro split was the winner.

The Roast smoked reverse sear — the thick end

  1. Score the fat cap in a diamond crosshatch; season all over.
  2. Smoke at 225°F with a probe in, to 115°F internal.
  3. Rest tented while the coals get screaming hot.
  4. Sear hard on all sides, fat cap first, to 125–130°F.
  5. Rest again, then slice against the grain.

Verdict: amazing — the scored squares go full bark, like a brisket crust, with the fat rendered into the grooves. Runs a touch under for medium tastes; see the split-doneness trick below.

Steak 1 chimichurri grill — the center

  1. Salt well. Mark the grain direction in your head before it cooks.
  2. Grill fat-cap down first to crisp the cap, then flip once. Pull at 125–130°F.
  3. Rest it in a puddle of chimichurri.
  4. Slice against the grain; spoon more chimichurri over the top.

Verdict: the star of the day. Delicious — pink edge to edge under a dark crust.

Steak 2 churrasco skewer — the tip, folded into a C

  1. Fold the piece into a C with the fat cap facing out, and run a wide skewer through it.
  2. Coarse salt only. Over the coals, turning a quarter-turn at a time.
  3. When a face is browned and crisp, shave thin slices off that face — a strip is 2–3 thin slices. Salt the fresh face and send it back to the fire.
  4. Never slice a full ring off: the inside of the curl is still rare.

Verdict: good — but it ran a bit well done when the grill flared (see lessons). The shave-off style forgives a lot.

The timeline so all three land together

  1. T-2:00 — make the chimichurri; it needs its rest.
  2. T-1:30 — roast goes on the smoker at 225°F.
  3. Roast hits 115°F — pull it to rest tented; light the coals.
  4. Skewer on first — it wants the most passes over the fire.
  5. Steak 1 next, then the roast sears last — it is already cooked through the middle.
  6. Slice the roast and Steak 1 while the skewer keeps making its rounds.

Lessons from the first cook Nov 27, 2026 — the flaming monster edition

  • Two-zone fire. Bank the coals to one side so the skewer has a cool parking spot. When Steak 1's fat cap rendered into a single-zone fire, the kettle became a flaming monster — and the thin tip piece, with no margin, cooked through it.
  • Sequence matters. Give the skewer its first shave round before Steak 1 goes fat-cap down, or park the skewer on the cool side while the cap renders.
  • Flare-ups: lid on for ten seconds chokes them. Better than chasing the meat around the grate.
  • Split-doneness trick: for anyone who likes it a step more done, flash their slices on the grill for a few seconds a side. Their slices finish; yours stay perfect.
  • Never past medium — picanha tightens up after 135°F.
