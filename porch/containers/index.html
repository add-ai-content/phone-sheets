berry patch 1

Orange lantana

Lantana camara

berry patch

Hummingbird and butterfly magnet. Leaves and unripe berries are toxic to dogs—deadhead if pets graze. Skip heavy fertilizer; extra nitrogen makes leaves, not flowers. Light monthly bloom feed in a pot is enough. Prefers lean, fast-draining mix.

NTX care
sun
Full sun, 6–8+ hours. West/south porch is fine; it blooms harder in NTX heat than in shade.
water
Pots dry the same day in 95–105°F. Soak when the top 1–2 in are dry—often daily in July–Aug on a west porch. Drain fully; never leave the pot in a saucer. Established plants want a wet-dry cycle, not constant wet mix.
heatNote
Built for 95–105°F and afternoon scorch; flowers peak in July–August. Black plastic pots cook roots—use a large (5 gal+), light-colored pot and lift it off hot concrete. Leaf wilt at noon that recovers by evening is normal; limp next morning means water now.
plantWhen
After last frost, mid- to late March through May. Can set a pot out in summer if you keep it watered the first 2 weeks.
overwinter
Zone 8b tender perennial. Tops die at first hard freeze. A pot is colder than ground—move to an unheated garage after frost, water barely, or treat as an annual and replace in April. Roots sometimes resprout if the pot is insulated and not left on a slab all winter.
prune
Snip spent clusters every couple of weeks to cut berry load and keep bloom coming. Midsummer shear of leggy tips by a few inches if it sprawls.
hardPrune
If it overwinters, wait until late Feb–March after the last hard freeze, then cut dead wood to 4–6 in. Do not prune in fall.

pool room window 2

Hardy rose mallow

Hibiscus moscheutos

pool room window

Native wetland perennial sold as dinner-plate hardy hibiscus. Huge magenta flowers, broad toothed leaves. Thirsty in containers; late to emerge—do not discard the pot in April. Japanese beetles and whiteflies possible. Large pot (at least 5 gal) and rich mix.

NTX care
sun
Morning sun + afternoon shade on the porch. Full all-day sun in a black pot will scorch leaves and fade blooms in 95–105°F NTX heat.
water
Keep the mix evenly moist. In July–August a 5-gal pot often dries the same day—water deeply every morning and again if the rim is light and the plant flags by mid-afternoon. Never let it bone-dry.
heatNote
Loves heat if the pot stays wet. Afternoon west sun plus a hot pot wall wilts it fast; move to east/morning light or set the pot in a saucer of water on 100°F days. Blooms last one day and look crumpled by evening even when healthy.
plantWhen
April–early May after last frost, or anytime the pot is already growing. Slow to wake in spring—wait for warm soil before expecting new shoots.
overwinter
Dies to the crown after freeze. Zone 8b pot can stay outside against a wall with mulch over the mix; slide into an unheated garage if a multi-night teens freeze is forecast. Water only enough so the root ball does not go dust-dry.
prune
Deadhead spent one-day blooms. Pinch tips once at 6–10 in in late spring for a bushier pot.
hardPrune
After hard frost (or late Feb before new shoots), cut dead stems to 4–6 in stubs so you can find the crown in spring.

Plumeria

Plumeria rubra (and hybrids)

pool room window

Deciduous in our winters even indoors if you withhold water. Flowers on new wood after heat builds; a leafy stick in September is normal. Watch for rust (orange underside specks) in humid spells—improve air and avoid wetting leaves late in the day. Stake young trunks in porch wind.

NTX care
sun
Full sun on the porch; morning-to-midday is ideal. West-wall afternoon in 95–105°F can bleach or crisp new leaves—shift the pot a few feet into bright shade after 2pm if the west face cooks.
water
In NTX summer pots dry the same day. Soak thoroughly when the top 2–3 in are dry and the pot feels light; drain fast. Do not keep it soggy. Cut water hard once nights cool and leaves start dropping.
heatNote
Loves 95–105°F heat if the pot is not bone-dry for days and roots are not sitting in hot, wet mix. Black plastic pots on brick will cook roots—use a larger pot, light-colored container, or a saucer lift. Leaf scorch is sun + drought, not heat alone.
plantWhen
Move outdoors after nights stay above ~55°F and soil in the pot is warm (typically mid-April to early May in 76182). Wait on new growth before heavy water.
overwinter
Not hardy here. Before first freeze, bring the pot into a garage, spare room, or bright indoor spot. Let it go leafless and keep almost dry all winter. No freeze, no soggy mix. Roll back out after last frost.
prune
Best after flowering or in spring as growth starts. Cut just above a node; white sap is normal (irritating—gloves). Shape for a stout trunk that fits the winter storage spot.
hardPrune
Yes—cut the whole head back to 12–24 in of trunk in fall if you need a compact dormant stick for the garage. It will leaf and branch from old wood in spring.

north bubbler 2

Cigar plant ‘David Verity’

Cuphea ignea ‘David Verity’

north bubbler

Hummingbird magnet. Plants For Texas tag. Opposite leaves, red stems, orange tubes with a yellow-orange flare. Needs drainage; wet feet rot it faster than drought. Spider mites possible on a hot, dusty porch—rinse foliage when you water.

NTX care
sun
Full morning sun; light afternoon shade in 95–105°F. West-facing pots cook leaves and dry mix by midafternoon.
water
Check daily in summer. Pots often dry the same day. Water when the top inch is dry; soak until it runs out. Do not leave the pot sitting in a saucer in heat.
heatNote
Handles NTX heat if roots stay evenly moist and the pot is not black plastic in raw west sun. Crispy leaf edges and dropped buds mean the mix baked dry, not that the plant ‘can’t take heat.’
plantWhen
After last frost (mid–late March in 76182) through early June. Fall pots still bloom until first hard freeze.
overwinter
Root-hardy in some 8b winters if the pot is large and sheltered; foliage dies at freeze. Safer: move the pot to an unheated garage or bright indoor window before a hard freeze; water sparingly. Or treat as an annual and replace in spring.
prune
Pinch tips in spring and early summer to keep it bushy. Shear lightly after a midsummer slump for a fall flush. No deadheading required.
hardPrune
After frost or in late winter, cut dead or woody stems to 4–6 in. Do not hard-cut a heat-stressed pot in July–August.

Firecracker plant

Russelia equisetiformis

north bubbler

Texas Superstar hummingbird plant. Stems cascade; stake or let it fountain over the pot rim. Bigger pot = fewer same-day waterings. Small leaves drop as stems mature—normal, not a nutrient crash.

NTX care
sun
Full sun to light afternoon shade. Morning sun is fine; west-wall 3–6pm in July–August can bleach flowers and cook the pot.
water
Check daily in 95–105°F. Pots often dry the same day. Water when the top inch is dry until it runs out the holes. Do not let it sit in a saucer. Established plants forgive a miss better than most porch annuals, but a bone-dry 2-gal still wilts hard.
heatNote
Handles NTX heat if the root ball is not baked. Dark pots on concrete get lethal by midafternoon—lift off the slab, use a cachepot, or slide to morning-sun/afternoon-shade. Bloom holds in heat if water and drainage stay honest.
plantWhen
After last frost (mid-April 76182). Nursery pots anytime frost is gone. Cuttings root easy in warm months.
overwinter
Tender in 8b. Move the pot into an unheated garage or bright indoor window before first freeze, or cut to 6–8 in and mulch hard against a south wall and hope for a mild year. Often treated as a summer pot and replaced.
prune
Snip spent racemes and weak tips through summer to keep it blooming and from flopping off the stake. Light shape anytime.
hardPrune
If you overwinter it, cut back hard in late February–March to 6–12 in before new growth. Outdoors after a freeze, cut dead canes to live wood or the crown.

Bat-face cuphea

Cuphea llavea

south bubbler

Mexican subshrub used as a long-bloom pot annual/perennial here. Red ‘ears’ and purple ‘face’ on hairy tubes. Needs rich, fast-draining mix; root rot if soggy. Feed lightly in-season. Whiteflies/aphids possible if brought indoors. Photo leaf burn is typical NTX pot stress, not a different species.

NTX care
sun
Morning sun + afternoon shade in July–Sept. Full sun is fine in spring and fall; west-facing black pots cook foliage and flowers at 95–105°F.
water
Check daily in summer. Pots often dry the same day. Water when the top inch is dry until it runs out the holes. Do not leave the pot in a full saucer.
heatNote
Heat-tolerant but pots wilt and scorch by mid-afternoon in 100°F+ west sun. Leaf-edge burn is common when the mix bakes. Move to morning sun or a brighter shade porch in peak heat. Hummingbirds still work the flowers if it is not crispy.
plantWhen
After last frost (mid–late March in 76182) through May. Can set a nursery pot anytime frost is gone.
overwinter
Borderline 8b in a pot. Roots may survive mid-20s if the pot is against a south wall and wrapped; foliage dies below ~28–30°F. Safer: roll indoors to a bright window, keep cool (~60s), water sparingly. Or treat as annual and replace in spring.
prune
Pinch tips when 8–10 in tall for a denser pot. Shear lightly midsummer if it gets lanky. No need to deadhead every bloom.
hardPrune
If it overwinters outdoors, cut dead wood to live stems after last frost. Do not hard-cut in fall.

Flame Acanthus 'Benny's Gold'

Anisacanthus quadrifidus var. wrightii 'Benny's Gold'

south bubbler

Gold-flower selection of Texas native flame acanthus; hummingbirds work the tubes. Tag misspells the genus/species. Peeling bark on older wood. Host for Texas crescentspot. Deer usually leave it alone. Will seed; gold color may not come true from seed.

NTX care
sun
Full sun for bloom; morning sun + light afternoon shade is safer on a west/south porch when pots bake 95–105°F. Scorch and fewer flowers in deep shade.
water
Drought-tough in ground, not in a pot. In NTX summer check daily—pots can dry the same day. Water when the top 1–2 in are dry; soak until it runs out. Do not keep soggy.
heatNote
Handles 95–105°F heat if the root ball is not bone-dry. Afternoon west-wall bounce can crisp tips; stake tall bloom spikes. Rain or a deep soak often triggers a new gold flush.
plantWhen
Spring after last frost, or fall once nights cool. Large pot (at least 14–18 in) with fast-drain mix.
overwinter
Zone 8b: tops often die back; roots usually live. Leave pot out against a wall or roll to a protected porch. Cut dead wood in late winter. Keep barely moist, never wet.
prune
Deadhead spent spikes to keep it blooming. Light shape after a flush. Stems are brittle—don't yank.
hardPrune
Late Feb–early March, before new growth: cut dead or all stems to 4–8 in if it froze to the ground. It resprouts from the crown.

Texas Star hibiscus

Hibiscus coccineus

south bubbler

Deeply palmate ‘star’ leaves + 5-petal scarlet bloom with long column. Hummingbirds and swallowtails use it. Self-sows if pods ripen. Not tropical H. rosa-sinensis—do not treat as a winter houseplant unless you want a lanky indoor experiment. Japanese beetles, whitefly, and spider mites show up in hot dry porch air; rinse undersides. Big drinker: pairing with a self-watering reservoir or a glazed pot helps July.

NTX care
sun
Morning sun + bright light; slide the pot out of 3–7pm west/south blast. Full all-day sun on black pavement cooks the crown and fades blooms. Aim 6 hours, not 10 in July.
water
Swamp plant in a pot: check morning and late afternoon. In 95–105°F it often needs a deep soak the same day it looks fine at breakfast. Never let the mix go bone-dry or buds abort. Drain hole required; saucer only in heat waves, dump standing water overnight.
heatNote
Handles NTX heat if roots stay wet and afternoon scorch is cut. Leaves wilt by 2pm in a small pot even when soil is damp—that is heat, not always drought. Larger (16–20 in+) pot, light-colored container, and a little shade cloth beat leaf crisp. Flowers last one day.
plantWhen
After last frost (mid–late March in 76182) through May. Avoid new transplants in July–August unless you can water twice a day and give temporary afternoon shade.
overwinter
Herbaceous hardy 8b. Tops blacken at first hard freeze; roots usually live in a large pot if not bone-dry and not sitting in ice water. Group against the house or roll into an unheated garage on single-digit nights. Keep mix barely moist all winter. Late to wake—often May.
prune
Deadhead spent one-day blooms if you want fewer seed pods. Stake tall stems before they flop. Do not cut live green stems in summer.
hardPrune
After several freezes and stems are brown/hollow (Dec–Feb), cut to 4–8 in stubs so you can find the crown. New wood only—spring hard-prune after growth starts costs that year’s flowers.

Trumpet vine

Campsis radicans

south bubbler

Hummingbird magnet. In a pot it is far less invasive than in soil, but it still wants a sturdy stake and will throw basal shoots—twist off extras. Sap and leaflets can irritate skin (cow-itch). Do not plant this one in the ground near gutters or siding unless you want a takeover.

NTX care
sun
Full morning sun; afternoon shade or bright filtered light in a pot. West-facing 95–105°F scorch bleaches flowers and cooks the root ball the same day.
water
Check daily in summer. Water when the top inch is dry; pots often need a deep soak every morning in 100°F heat. Do not let it wilt to bone-dry, then sit soggy.
heatNote
Heat-tough once established, but a black pot in afternoon sun can stall bloom and drop leaves. Mulch the surface, use a light-colored pot if you can, and keep the vine on a stake/trellis so foliage does not cook on hot concrete.
plantWhen
Set out after last frost (mid-March–April in 76182) or pot up anytime frost-free. Best new growth and first bloom the following summer.
overwinter
Zone 8b hardy. Leave outdoors; move the pot against a wall and keep soil barely moist in freezes. Roots in a pot run colder than in-ground—wrap the container or group pots if a hard Arctic blast is forecast. Deciduous; bare woody canes are normal.
prune
Deadhead spent clusters if you want a tidier pot. Lightly thin after a flush. Blooms on new wood, so do not shear hard in summer if you want more trumpets.
hardPrune
Late winter (Feb) cut canes back hard to 12–24 in or to a few strong buds. In a pot this keeps it a shrub-on-a-stake instead of a fence-eater.

Turk's Cap 'Big Momma'

Malvaviscus × 'Big Momma' (M. drummondii × M. penduliflorus)

south bubbler

Greg Grant Texas hybrid: native hardiness + larger drooping red flowers. Hummingbirds work the unopened tubes. Marble-size red fruits are edible (mealy apple). Bigger and thirstier in a pot than straight native drummondii.

NTX care
sun
Morning sun + afternoon shade in pots. East or dappled porch is safest. Full west/south sun in a black pot will scorch leaves in July–August.
water
In 95–105°F NTX heat a pot dries the same day. Water thoroughly every morning; check again late afternoon. Do not let it wilt on repeat or bloom stalls.
heatNote
Heat-tough once roots fill the pot, but container soil hits lethal temps on concrete and west walls. Afternoon shade or a bigger, light-colored pot is the difference between a hummingbird plant and fried leaves.
plantWhen
April–May after last freeze, or September so roots set before first hard freeze. Skip midsummer transplant into a hot pot.
overwinter
Hardy in-ground in 8b (dies to the crown, returns). In a pot the roots freeze faster — group against the house, wrap/mulch the container, or roll into the garage on nights in the teens. Do not leave a small pot exposed on the driveway.
prune
Pinch tips in late spring for a bushier pot plant. Cut spent stems anytime. No need to deadhead for rebloom.
hardPrune
After frost kill or in late February before new shoots: cut to 4–8 in. or to live wood. New canes come from the base.

Flame acanthus

Anisacanthus quadrifidus var. wrightii

central bubbler

Texas native hummingbird/butterfly magnet. Blooms June–frost, often in flushes after rain. Large pot with drainage; lean, fast-draining mix. Little fertilizer. Seeds out in the yard if happy. Deer usually leave it. Brittle stems—don't crowd it where it gets bumped.

NTX care
sun
Full sun for bloom; morning sun + light afternoon shade is safer on west/south porch walls that hit 95–105°F. Deep shade = few tubes.
water
In-ground it is drought-tough; in a pot it can bone-dry the same day in July–August. Soak when the top 1–2 in are dry. Do not keep soggy. Extra water in heat = more flowers; constant wet = rot.
heatNote
Loves NTX heat and reflected pavement once roots fill the pot. Afternoon scorch browns leaf tips in small black nursery cans; step up to a larger, light-colored pot and do not let it wilt hard on 100°F days.
plantWhen
Spring after last frost through early fall. Best pot-up: March–May or September in 76182 so roots set before peak heat or first hard freeze.
overwinter
USDA 8b root-hardy. Tops often freeze and drop leaves after a hard frost. Leave the pot outdoors in a sheltered spot, or slide against the house. Mulch the soil surface. Water only enough that the mix does not go bone-dry for weeks.
prune
Deadhead spent tubes if you want a tidier look; not required. Light shear after a bloom flush to keep it from getting lanky in the pot.
hardPrune
Late winter to early spring (Feb–March), after you see what froze. Cut dead wood to live green, or cut nearly to the crown if it died to the soil. New shoots come from the base; do not hard-prune in fall.

Honeybells Cuphea

Cuphea hybrid 'Balhoelrel'

central bubbler

Hummingbird magnet; tubular rose-red flowers with yellow throats, no deadheading needed. Spreading/trailing filler, 10–12 in tall. Good drainage mix; overwater + heat = collapse. Photos show late-season pot stress—same-day water and afternoon shade will green it back.

NTX care
sun
Morning sun + afternoon shade in NTX. Tag says sun/part shade; west/south porch after 2pm in 95–105°F will scorch leaves and cook black-plastic roots. Bright east or filtered west is the pot sweet spot.
water
Pots dry the same day in July–August. Finger-check daily; soak until water runs out when the top inch is dry. Never leave the pot in a full saucer. Wilted tubes recover after a deep drink if roots are not cooked.
heatNote
Heat-tolerant bloomer, not drought-proof in a pot. 95–105°F plus reflected driveway heat wilts fast and burns tender new tips. Mulch the soil surface, use a light-colored pot if you can, and skip midafternoon sun.
plantWhen
After last frost when nights stay above about 55°F (mid–late April typical in 76182). Can set out a bought pot now and keep watering through first frost.
overwinter
Tender in USDA 8b. Treat as annual or roll the pot indoors before nights hit the mid-40s. Bright window, water sparingly, no fertilizer until spring. Outdoor survival is a gamble below the mid-20s.
prune
Pinch tips in spring and after a flush to keep it bushy. Snip spent or crispy stems anytime. Do not shear hard during a 100°F week.
hardPrune
Only if overwintered: cut back dead wood in March as new growth shows. Do not hard-prune a heat-stressed pot in midsummer.

Texas sage

Leucophyllum frutescens

central bubbler

Gritty, fast-drain mix (cactus/succulent + extra grit). Low fertilizer. Silver leaves + fuzzy purple trumpets after storms. Same-day pot dry-down is normal; soggy nights are not.

NTX care
sun
Full sun all day. Needs 8+ hours; afternoon shade makes it sparse and bloom-shy.
water
Soak, then let the mix go dry several inches down before the next drink. In a 95–105°F pot it can dry the same day—check weight, don’t water on a calendar. Empty the saucer. Overwater kills it faster than drought.
heatNote
Built for NTX heat; foliage handles 105°F. The pot is the weak point: black plastic cooks roots. Use a large pot, light color or clay, lift off pavement. Blooms after humidity/rain spikes, not from extra water.
plantWhen
Fall (Oct–Nov) or late Feb–March. Avoid midsummer transplant unless you can shade the pot for 7–10 days.
overwinter
Hardy 8b; leave outdoors. Brief teens may burn tips. Keep the mix on the dry side in freezes. Wrap the pot, not the plant, if a hard Arctic blast is forecast.
prune
Light shape after a bloom flush or late Feb–early March. Do not shear into a tight ball—flowers on new wood and shearing hollows the interior.
hardPrune
If woody or ragged: late winter, cut oldest stems back by up to one-third. Don’t scalp to stubs every year.
